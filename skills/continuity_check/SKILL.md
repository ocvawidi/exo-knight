# Skill: Continuity Check

Status: PROVISIONAL workflow specification. A runtime must explicitly load and implement it.

## Purpose
Detect material contradictions, unsupported assumptions, and state drift before a scene, recap, character action, or proposed lore change is presented as consistent.

A continuity check validates against available evidence; it does not create missing canon or guarantee correctness when relevant sources are unavailable.

## Use when
- Before continuing a scene after retrieving a checkpoint or prior context.
- Reviewing a scene, recap, character action, or proposed lore change.
- A character uses an ability, tool, piece of knowledge, resource, or relationship fact that affects the scene.
- A time skip, location change, injury, equipment change, promise, or major event occurs.
- Two sources or turns appear to disagree.
- The creator asks whether something is consistent with established lore.

For routine low-impact exchanges, use a proportionate check rather than a long report.

## Evidence and precedence
1. Follow the creator's latest explicit instruction for the current task, unless it conflicts with a higher-priority safety or system constraint.
2. Treat creator-approved canon and designated authoritative project records as the basis for established world facts.
3. Use the latest reliable session checkpoint for the last recorded session state, while checking it against newer conversation turns.
4. Treat confirmed events in the current conversation as session facts; do not automatically promote them to permanent canon.
5. Treat PROVISIONAL and PROPOSED details as tentative, and UNKNOWN details as unestablished.
6. If two sources conflict or the source authority is unclear, report the conflict and identify both sources. Do not silently choose one or rewrite canon.

A newer statement does not automatically override an approved canon source unless the creator clearly approves a canon change.

## Check procedure
1. **Define scope.** Identify the scene or claim being checked and the consequences if it is wrong.
2. **Gather relevant evidence.** Use the available approved lore, character sheets, checkpoint, and recent turns. Do not claim to have consulted unavailable files or history.
3. **Build a minimal state snapshot.** Note relevant time, location, present characters, character knowledge, capabilities, condition/resources, relationships, and open commitments.
4. **Compare the draft or event.** Check each relevant dimension below against the evidence.
5. **Classify each finding.** Use PASS, WARNING, or CONFLICT. Include UNKNOWN where the missing information is itself material.
6. **Choose the least disruptive next step.** Keep the detail, revise the draft, ask the creator/player, or label the detail PROPOSED.
7. **Protect player agency.** Remove narration that decides the player's unchosen actions, dialogue, decisions, consent, or private thoughts.
8. **Report proportionately.** For a clean routine scene, a short internal check or concise PASS is enough. Explain material warnings and conflicts clearly.

## Check dimensions
- **Timeline:** Do dates, event order, elapsed time, and travel time agree with known facts?
- **Location:** Can each character plausibly be present, and is movement between locations accounted for?
- **Character knowledge:** How did each character learn the fact? Is the information available to them at this point in the timeline?
- **Capabilities:** Are powers, techniques, equipment, costs, conditions, and limitations supported by established sources?
- **Relationships:** Does the interaction conflict with confirmed relationship history, commitments, or boundaries?
- **State persistence:** Do injuries, fatigue, equipment, resources, and other relevant state changes persist unless a recovery or change is established?
- **Character identity and voice:** Are names, roles, speech patterns, and established traits consistent with available evidence?
- **Open threads:** Are unresolved promises, threats, mysteries, and pending decisions preserved rather than accidentally treated as settled?
- **Player agency:** Does the narration avoid deciding the player's actions, dialogue, choices, consent, or private thoughts?
- **Canon status:** Has a proposal, theory, or improvised detail been presented as canon without creator approval?
- **Language and format:** Is Indonesian the default for narration and dialogue, with English limited to established technical/in-world terms or brief illustrative interface/status messages unless another language is requested?

## Result levels
- **PASS:** No material continuity issue found within the evidence checked. This does not mean every fact in the wider project was verified.
- **WARNING:** A possible issue exists, evidence is incomplete, or a detail is UNKNOWN and may affect the scene.
- **CONFLICT:** Available sources directly contradict a material detail.
- **NOT CHECKED:** The required source, context, or capability was unavailable. Do not imply verification occurred.

## Report format
Use this concise structure when a report is useful:

- **Result:** PASS / WARNING / CONFLICT / NOT CHECKED
- **Finding:** The specific detail at issue.
- **Evidence:** The relevant turn, checkpoint, or project file and its status.
- **Impact:** What may become inconsistent if left unresolved.
- **Recommended next step:** Keep, revise, ask, or mark as PROPOSED.

Do not manufacture citations or file references. Identify source paths or conversation context only when actually available.

## Resolution rules
- If the issue is a clear drafting mistake and correction does not change canon or player intent, revise the draft.
- If a nonessential detail is unknown, leave it unspecified.
- If an unknown detail materially affects the next scene, ask a focused question or mark a clearly labeled proposal.
- If approved canon conflicts with session improvisation, preserve approved canon and flag the improvisation for correction or creator review.
- If two authoritative sources conflict, surface both and request creator direction rather than silently selecting one.
- If a player-controlled decision or action is missing, stop at the decision point and let the player choose.
- Never invent an explanation, new ability, retcon, timestamp, or off-screen event solely to conceal a continuity problem.

## Do not
- Treat absence of evidence as proof that something did not happen.
- Treat a checkpoint as more authoritative than approved canon.
- Assume an improvised event is canon merely because it appears in a recap.
- Attribute knowledge, motives, emotions, or off-screen actions without support.
- Resolve open threads or pending decisions just to make the story tidy.
- Claim a check passed when the necessary context was not available.

## Quality checklist
Before finalizing the scene or report, verify:
- [ ] Relevant sources were actually available and consulted.
- [ ] Current conversation turns were checked against older checkpoint state.
- [ ] Findings are classified proportionately and tied to evidence.
- [ ] Unknowns and conflicts are visible, not silently filled in.
- [ ] Player agency and canon approval boundaries are preserved.
- [ ] Character knowledge, abilities, and state changes remain supported.
- [ ] Indonesian remains the default language unless the creator requests otherwise.
